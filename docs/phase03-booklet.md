## 01 · Context — From Reality to Resilience

A massive global cyber disaster in the year 2065, caused by the Super Malware Agent, has taken down systems across governments, businesses, healthcare, transportation, and financial services. The entire world has been brought to a standstill.

In the financial sector, the damage is severe. Core banking systems, ATMs, digital payment platforms, and loan systems have completely stopped working. Millions of people have lost access to essential financial services. Small businesses can no longer process digital transactions, and people are forced to rely entirely on cash. This loss of secure banking has slowed economic recovery and increased financial inequality across the world.

The Master Key has been recovered and the banking platform has been rebuilt. Customer data is safe, the services are written, and the architecture holds. But a platform that only runs inside a repository saves no one. Millions of people are still locked out of their money, and the world is waiting for the banks to come back online.

**The mission of Duothan 6.0 is to save the digital banking system.**

Deploying that platform to the cloud, releasing it through automation, and operating it as a service the world can depend on, running as independent services.

A working application is not a restored financial system. The platform must survive real traffic, real failures, and real attackers. Every release must be repeatable, every environment reproducible, every secret protected, and every incident visible long before a customer feels it.

**Your mission is to take the system out of the repository and into production, and to prove it can stay there.**

## 02 · Brief — Phase Overview

In Phase 3 - Restore, teams move from a working application to a running production system. This phase focuses on deploying the banking platform built during Phase 2 onto a cloud environment as a set of independent services, and on building the automation around it that makes every release repeatable, verifiable, and safe. The deployed solution should remain consistent with the architecture proposed in Phase 1 and the functionality delivered in Phase 2, now operating as a live and publicly accessible service.

Teams are expected to move their solution out of the development environment and into a live, reliable service. The platform should:

- Behave the same way wherever it runs
- Recover from failure without manual intervention
- Remain available as demand changes
- Allow confident and repeatable releases
- Provide visibility into the state of the running system at any moment rather than discovering problems through users

Security and engineering discipline are expected throughout the delivery process rather than at the end of it. Every change should reach production through the same controlled and repeatable route, with risks identified before release and sensitive information protected at every stage.

Teams are free to decide how they meet the requirements, provided their approach follows industry best practices and can be justified during evaluation.
